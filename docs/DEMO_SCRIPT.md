# Kannadada Beru — 4-minute live demo

## Opening · 0:00–0:25

“ಪ್ರತಿಯೊಂದು ನುಡಿಗೂ ಕಥೆಯಿದೆ. ಪ್ರತಿಯೊಂದು ಕಥೆಗೂ ನೆಲವಿದೆ.”

Kannada does not sound identical everywhere it lives. A familiar word can carry a different form, rhythm, memory, or cultural setting from one district to another. Kannadada Beru helps people discover that diversity without separating a word from its place, people, meaning, or evidence.

## 1. Start with living voices · 0:25–1:05

From the homepage, open **Discover regional voices**.

This is the heart of the product: one focused place where a visitor can search source-documented regional usages, filter them by region, and understand each expression in Kannada, Romanized Kannada, and English. Every card keeps the local form beside its standard comparison, locality, cultural context, and source.

Point out the evidence ledger. Regional Kannada sound is not manufactured or guessed. It appears only when the recording, transcript, speaker locality, permission, and community review all match. The public Tulu recording is deliberately presented as Tulu—not mislabeled as a Kannada dialect.

## 2. Explore Karnataka · 1:05–1:40

Open **Explore** and select any district on the map.

The atlas covers all 31 current Karnataka districts. A district is more than a link to a government website: its page gives a bilingual introduction to history, local character, places worth exploring, official references, and a path back into the relevant language region. The map is a doorway into learning, not a claim that administrative borders are dialect boundaries.

Open coastal Karnataka briefly and show how Tulu is acknowledged as a distinct Dravidian language central to the region’s living culture.

## 3. Search the dictionary · 1:40–2:15

Open **Dictionary** and search in Kannada, Romanized Kannada, or English.

The app provides 156,650 Kannada–English entries derived from the open Alar dictionary. Instead of forcing a large download, it loads one of 50 Kannada-initial shards only when needed. That keeps the experience fast on ordinary phones while preserving direct source attribution.

## 4. Compare carefully · 2:15–2:45

Open **Compare** and place two regions side by side.

The comparison does not invent one-to-one equivalents. It shows geography, culture, documented usages, sources, districts, and available voice counts together. Vocabulary differences, pronunciation differences, read speech, and spontaneous speech remain separate concepts rather than being flattened into a single “dialect” label.

## 5. Learn through play · 2:45–3:15

Open **Pada Beru**.

The ten-round mini-game turns dictionary discovery into a quick Kannada–English meaning challenge with a score, streak, hints, and optional device pronunciation. It gives younger visitors and first-time learners a memorable way to return to the language.

## 6. Contribute without losing control · 3:15–3:40

Open **Contribute**.

A visitor can prepare a structured local-language record with its meaning, place, context, and permission details. The draft stays on the device and nothing is silently uploaded. The contributor chooses when to export it for review. This makes community participation understandable and privacy-conscious.

## Closing · 3:40–4:00

Kannadada Beru is already a complete, installable public experience: 31 district stories, a 156,650-entry dictionary, regional discovery, careful comparison, real attributed audio, a game, and a private contribution workspace. It is built for Android-sized and desktop screens and verified through the full public journey.

Our goal is simple: let every person from Karnataka find a familiar word, understand the land and people around it, and—when permission and evidence are ready—hear a real community voice.

**ನಮ್ಮ ನುಡಿ · ನಮ್ಮ ಬೇರು · ನಮ್ಮ ಹೆಮ್ಮೆ.**
